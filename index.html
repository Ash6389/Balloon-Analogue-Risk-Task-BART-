Classroom replication of Pei et al. (2020)

Balloon Analogue Risk Task

The risk game 83 teenagers played inside an fMRI scanner. Pump the balloon to raise its value, and bank before it bursts.

How it works

  • Each Inflate makes the balloon bigger and adds points to its wager.
  • The balloon could explode at any size. Bigger balloons are worth more and are more likely to explode.
  • If it explodes, you lose that balloon's wager.
  • Bank adds the wager to your total. Banked points are safe, then a new balloon appears.

About this task

In Pei et al. (2020), 16–17-year-old males played this BART during fMRI. One week later they drove a simulator alone and with a risky or a cautious peer passenger. Brain responses to bigger stakes in this game predicted who followed the risky peer.

  • Up to 12 pumps per balloon. The balloon's value rises from 0 to 5.15 points, and the first pumps are worth 0.05, 0.15 and 0.25, as in the paper's Figure 1.
  • Changed from the paper: every balloon has a hidden burst point somewhere between pump 3 and pump 12, and about 1 in 10 balloons never bursts. Burst points are spread out and shuffled, so they do not cluster around one size.
  • Lab timing uses a random 1.5–2.5 s pause between decisions on a balloon and 2–4 s between balloons.
  • The risk score is the adjusted pumps: average pumps on balloons you banked. The study's mean was 4.70 (SD 0.86).
